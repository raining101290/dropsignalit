About

Biography

We're DropSignal It. We help people learn how to learn.

DropSignal It began in a small town where the library closed at four and the internet came in dial-up whispers. What our founder lacked in resources was made up for with curiosity, and a stubborn belief that anything could be learned if you broke it down small enough.

In 2013, armed with a borrowed laptop and a cheap microphone, we recorded our first tutorial for a handful of university friends. It reached ten thousand people in a month. That accident became a calling: to make world-class learning feel personal, practical and within reach.

Since then we've published six books, built eighteen courses and grown into an independent education company serving learners in more than ninety countries. We still write every morning, still answer reader emails, and still believe the best teachers never stop being students.

The journey

Twelve years, one lesson at a time.

  1. 2013

    The first tutorial

    Recorded a study-skills video for friends. It reached 10,000 students in a month and changed everything.

  2. 2016

    Left the day job

    Went full-time on teaching after the first course crossed 5,000 students, with six months of savings and a lot of nerve.

  3. 2018

    The Sunday Letter

    Started a weekly newsletter with 212 subscribers. It's now read by more than 120,000 people every week.

  4. 2020

    First book, first bestseller

    Built to Teach was published and spent 14 weeks at the top of its category.

  5. 2023

    DropSignal It Studio

    Built a small, remote team of eleven to design cohort programs and scholarships for first-generation learners.

  6. 2026

    250,000 learners

    Crossed a quarter-million students and launched a free learning path for teachers in low-income schools.

Personal philosophy

Five principles we try to live by.

Depth over breadth.

Most of the value in life comes from a few things done exceptionally well. We'd rather master one skill than dabble in twenty.

Teach to learn.

Explaining an idea is the fastest way to discover what you don't understand. Every lesson we teach, we learn twice.

Calm is a competitive advantage.

Sustainable pace beats frantic hustle. The work that lasts is built by people who can keep showing up.

Generosity compounds.

Give away your best ideas. The trust you build is worth far more than anything you could hold back.

Stay a beginner.

Curiosity is a muscle. Keep picking up things you're bad at, and your whole life stays interesting.
